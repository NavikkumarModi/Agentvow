## 1. HUMAN FACTORS

### 1.1 Automation bias / complacency (foundations)

**Parasuraman & Manzey, "Complacency and Bias in Human Use of Automation: An Attentional Integration"**
- Date: Human Factors 52(3), 2010. URL: https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Complacency-and-Bias-in-Human-Use-of-Automation:-An-Parasuraman-Manzey/b0e5a85803bb959ed2cbd47c51009cc48059c02c `[snippet]`
- Problem: why operators over-rely on decision aids. Mechanism: integrated attentional model; complacency arises under multi-task load; automation bias yields omission AND commission errors when aids are imperfect; found in naive and expert participants and not removed by simple practice (per snippet).
- Evidence source: review of prior experiments (not a new study). Relevance: grounds H2; omission errors (not noticing what the aid failed to flag) are exactly the Agentvow UNKNOWN/hidden-divergence case.
- Limitation: aviation/process-control domain, not code review. Differentiation: n/a (background).

**Parasuraman, Molloy & Singh (1993), "Performance consequences of automation-induced complacency"**
- URL: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930055574 `[snippet]`. Reported (snippet): failure detection ~82% when automation reliability varied vs ~33% when constant, after ~20 min, in multitask conditions; no effect in single-task. 
- Implication: consistently reliable verifiers breed complacency. Agentvow, if it is right 95% of the time, may induce the very complacency the thesis warns about. Study must test an imperfect-Agentvow arm.

**Goddard, Roudsari & Wyatt, "Automation bias: a systematic review of frequency, effect mediators, and mitigators" (JAMIA 2012)**
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3240751/ `[snippet, appeared in results, not opened]`. Clinical decision support; background only. Needs read for frequency figures.

### 1.2 Explanations and overreliance in human-AI decision-making

**Bansal et al., "Does the Whole Exceed its Parts? The Effect of AI Explanations on Complementary Team Performance" (CHI 2021)**
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14779 (arXiv PDF fetch failed to parse; landing/summary via search) `[snippet]`.
- Problem: do explanations make human+AI teams better than either alone. Mechanism: mixed-methods user studies on three datasets with AI of accuracy comparable to humans; conditions with/without explanations and confidence.
- Reported (snippet): complementary improvement observed on every task, NOT increased by explanations; explanations raised acceptance of the AI recommendation regardless of correctness; explanations offered no benefit vs. displaying AI confidence alone. I could not retrieve exact accuracy numbers or N; do not quote any.
- Relevance: directly relevant to "plain-language explanation of agent claims." Plausible-sounding explanation is a risk factor. Agentvow's view must not be "a more persuasive explanation" - it must present checkable evidence and UNKNOWN.
- Limitation: lab tasks (sentiment, recidivism-like, etc.), crowd workers; not code or agents.

**Buçinca, Malaya & Gajos, "To Trust or to Think: Cognitive Forcing Functions Can Reduce Overreliance on AI in AI-assisted Decision-making" (CSCW 2021)**
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692 `[read: abstract/landing]`. N=199.
- Result: cognitive forcing (e.g., make a decision before seeing AI, slow down, on-demand AI) significantly reduced overreliance vs simple explainable-AI approaches; but participants rated forcing designs less favorably and trusted them less (acceptability-effectiveness trade-off); benefits larger for people high in Need for Cognition (so inequitable). Exact effect sizes not extracted.
- Implication: forced independent judgment before reveal is the only mitigation here with replicated-style support, but at a usability cost. It directly conflicts with "without increasing review burden" in the research question; the study must measure that trade-off, not assume it away.

**Vasconcelos et al., "Explanations Can Reduce Overreliance on AI Systems During Decision-Making" (CSCW 2023)**
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06823 `[snippet]`. 5 studies, N=731, maze task with simulated AI. Cost-benefit framing: overreliance rises with task difficulty; explanations do not change overreliance in easy tasks and reduce it in hard tasks, when they lower the cost of verifying. Some prior null results may be due to explanations not lowering verification cost.
- Implication (key): Agentvow's value hypothesis is precisely "lower the cost of verification." So the study should vary task difficulty and show a cost-of-verification mechanism, not only accuracy. Limitation: simulated AI, maze task.

**Explanation-induced overreliance (general)**: Bansal 2021 plus the "Cognitive Forcing for Better Decision-Making: Reducing Overreliance on AI Systems Through Partial Explanations" paper (ACM, https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3710946 `[snippet]`) and "Decision Theoretic Framework for Measuring AI Reliance" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15356 `[snippet]`). The last is relevant to the metric: define appropriate reliance relative to a normative baseline rather than raw agreement.

### 1.3 Uncertainty communication and calibrated trust

- "I'm Not Sure, But...": uncertainty expression in LLM search (FAccT 2024), https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3630106.3658941 `[snippet]`: first-person uncertainty expressions reduced confidence and agreement and raised accuracy.
- Linguistic Uncertainty Markers for Trust Calibration (ACM, 2026), https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3816046.3816231 `[snippet]`: hedged and unhedged advice rated equally trustworthy, yet hedged advice followed less often (behavioural vs subjective dissociation).
- "More is not better: visual uncertainty cues and the fragility of trust calibration in LLM-assisted decision making" (ScienceDirect, 2026), https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949882126000587 `[snippet]`: visual confidence indicators improved subjective sensitivity but amplified agreement with incorrect outputs; combining verbal hedging and visual cues produced the highest overreliance on incorrect responses.
- "Not All Uncertainty Is Equal: How Uncertainty Granularity Shapes Human Verification" https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.28571 `[snippet]`.
- Implication: UNKNOWN presentation is itself an experimental variable. Do NOT assume that adding more uncertainty markers helps; test verbal-only vs visual-only vs combined, and measure behaviour, not stated trust. All `[snippet]`; need reads.

### 1.4 Approval / consent fatigue and HITL for agents

- **Anthropic, "How we contain Claude"** https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/how-we-contain-claude `[read]`: 93% of permission prompts approved; sandbox gave 84% fewer prompts; auto mode (a second model decides) catches ~83% of "overeager behaviors". Vendor telemetry, no methodology detail in the part I read. Important for positioning: the vendor's answer to fatigue is to move the decision to another LLM, which is the "agreement is not independent verification" pattern Agentvow critiques. Also confirms that LLM-in-the-gate is a competing paradigm.
- Auto mode made default for Pro/Max/Team on 14 Aug 2026 (DevOps.com, `[snippet]`, unverified).
- **Turan, "Oversight Has a Capacity: Calibrating Agent Guards to a Subjective, Fatiguing Human"** arXiv 2606.08919, 2026-06-08 `[read: abstract]`. 125 labelled agent actions, reviewers' Fleiss' kappa = 0.52 (moderate; no single correct label for risk); simulation (modelled, not human-measured) shows safety peaks at intermediate escalation rates (inverted U); flooding attack on fatigue. Open-source oversight system. Relevance: high; same problem framing (finite human attention). Differentiation: it calibrates when to escalate; Agentvow changes what the human sees (claim vs reality). Limitation: fatigue is modelled, not measured in humans. Study protocol should inherit: label ambiguity (kappa) implies ground truth for "decision-changing" must be defined by construction (injected, verifiable divergences), not rater opinion.
- **Mitchell, Ghosh & Passi, "AI Agents Push Humans Out of the Loop"** arXiv 2608.23642, 2026-08-24 `[read: abstract]`. Position paper; argues HITL designs undermine oversight and erode skills. No empirical effect sizes. Use as framing, not evidence.
- "Confirmation Fatigue and the Protocol Gap in Agentic AI Oversight" (blog, changkun.de) `[snippet]`; low evidentiary weight.
- **Agent PR review in the wild**: "These Aren't the Reviews You're Looking For: How Humans Review AI-Generated Pull Requests," arXiv 2605.02273 `[read: abstract/summary]`. On 33,596 agent PRs (AIDev): 61.38% had no recorded review activity; 15.9% had observable human participation; 71.58% of review comments were by AI agents vs 28.42% humans; 25.92% of human comments on agent PRs were "agent steering". Observational; absence of review recorded is not absence of human attention. Also: 46.41% of fixes by Copilot/Devin/Cursor/Claude rejected (arXiv 2606.13468 `[snippet]`).
- Controlled code-review: Crupi, Tufano, Bavota, ICPC'26, arXiv 2602.11925 `[read: landing]`; 29 developers; per snippet, giving an AI-generated review as a starting point made reviewers focus on the pointed locations and overlook other issues (anchoring). I confirmed the paper and N=29 but not the result wording from the paper text. Relevance: a claim-vs-reality view that highlights locations may narrow review scope. The study must include "off-highlight" seeded defects.
- Other `[snippet]`, unread: Codacy blog on automation bias in AI code review; "When Review Alone No Longer Scales: Layered Supervision" arXiv 2608.26316; "Rethinking Code Review in the Age of AI" arXiv 2605.17548. ### 1.6 Implications for Agentvow design
1. Do not rely on explanation quality. Bansal 2021 (snippet) says explanations raise acceptance regardless of correctness. Show evidence artifacts (hash-bound observations, diffs, command outputs) and the decision-relevant gap, and make the "what would change my mind" statement concrete.
2. UNKNOWN must be a first-class, visually and verbally distinct state, with its own tested presentation (uncertainty-cue literature shows verbal+visual combos can backfire).
3. Offer an optional cognitive-forcing mode ("state your expectation before seeing divergences") for high-stakes decisions; default off to respect burden. Buçinca shows it works but is disliked.
4. Avoid anchoring: show the full change surface summary (counts of unreviewed areas), not only flagged locations (Crupi et al. snippet).
5. Rate/volume discipline: limit interruptions to decision-changing divergences (matches DCRD) and track alert precision; Turan's inverted-U suggests more escalation can reduce safety.
6. Anti-complacency: periodically include honest signals of verifier limits (coverage, which collectors did not run); consistently-correct verifiers induce complacency (Parasuraman 1993, snippet).
7. Never let a verdict look like approval (human agency rule); avoid green "safe" states; use "no divergence found in N checks, M unknown".

### 1.7 Implications for the human-study protocol (what must be controlled)
- Conditions (minimum): (A) agent's own summary only; (B) agent summary + raw transcript/diff; (C) Agentvow view; (D) an "LLM reviewer verdict" arm (to test H7: reality-challenge vs model agreement and to mirror auto-mode style); (E) optionally Agentvow + forcing function. Between-subjects or Latin-square across tasks.
- Seeded ground truth: inject divergences with known, deterministic ground truth (claim says tests pass but stale; claim of no downstream impact but consumer breaks), including decision-irrelevant divergences (to measure over-flagging) and UNKNOWN cases (no resolvable evidence).
- Include Agentvow-wrong trials (false SUPPORTED, false CONTRADICTED) at a stated rate; otherwise complacency is unmeasured.
- Metrics: decision accuracy (approve/reject vs ground-truth-correct), harmful-approval rate, appropriate reliance per the decision-theoretic framing (arXiv 2401.15356 `[snippet]`) rather than raw agreement, time-to-decision, review burden (time, clicks, evidence opened, NASA-TLX-style workload), calibration of confidence, UNKNOWN handling (does the participant defer/investigate or approve?), and detection of off-highlight defects.
- Controls: participant expertise (professional developers vs students), Need for Cognition (Buçinca moderator), task difficulty (Vasconcelos: effects appear only on hard tasks), incentives (Incentive-Tuning, arXiv 2601.15064 `[snippet]`), base rate of bad agent output (prevalence effect on vigilance), volume/fatigue (include long sessions, e.g., 20+ decisions, with order and time-on-task as covariates), plain-language reading level, same underlying evidence across arms, and a pre-registered analysis plan. Define "decision-changing" before data collection; do not use rater opinion (kappa 0.52 in Turan).
- Report: effect sizes with CIs, per-condition false-approval and false-rejection rates, and the burden trade-off explicitly. Sample size must be powered for the smallest meaningful effect; literature effects here are mostly small/moderate and often null (explanations) so do not assume large.

### 4.2 MCP
- Current spec **2026-07-28** (stable) `[read]` https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/. Changes: stateless core (no initialize handshake or session IDs); Multi Round-Trip Requests (`resultType: "input_required"` for mid-call user input/confirmation); `Mcp-Method`/`Mcp-Name` headers for gateway routing; cacheable list results (`ttlMs`, `cacheScope`); auth hardening (RFC 9207 issuer validation, Client ID Metadata Documents replacing DCR); Tasks moved to extension `io.modelcontextprotocol/tasks`; formal extensions framework; Roots, Sampling, Logging deprecated (12-month minimum), legacy HTTP+SSE deprecated.
- Relevance: MCP is a collector surface (read-only MCP servers as evidence sources) and a claim source (tool-call results); header-based metering helps gateway-side observation; input_required flow is where a human approval could be surfaced. Limit: tool results are server-asserted; a hostile or buggy server's output is a claim, not evidence. Tool annotations (readOnlyHint etc.) are hints, `[prior]`.

### 4.3 A2A
- Google announced April 2025; transferred to Linux Foundation June 2025 `[read]` https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent2Agent (Wikipedia, secondary). Agent Cards, HTTP/JSON, TLS/JWT/OIDC. v1.0 reported in snippet (crystl.dev, harnessrouter.ai) `[snippet]`; verify at a2a-protocol.org before citing. Relevance: inter-agent messages are claims; governance gap per arXiv 2606.31498 (matrix, unread).

### 4.4 ACP: two distinct protocols (verified)
1. **IBM Agent Communication Protocol (BeeAI)**: IBM's page `[read]` states "ACP is now part of A2A under the Linux Foundation", BeeAI Framework as primary implementation, migration guide provided; no merger date on the page. Snippets put the merge around August 2025 `[snippet]`. This is what "ACP folded into A2A" refers to.
2. **Agent Client Protocol (Zed, editor-to-coding-agent)**: https://agentclientprotocol.com `[read]`: JSON-RPC over stdio for local agents, HTTP/WebSocket for remote (remote support WIP), reuses MCP JSON representations, Markdown for user-readable text. Created by Zed per snippets `[snippet]`; the docs page I fetched did not name the creator. Still active; not folded into A2A. Relevance: a plausible integration point for surfacing Agentvow findings in an editor and for observing editor-mediated permission requests (not confirmed in the page; check spec).
3. **Agent Control Protocol (arXiv 2603.18829, v1.27 Draft Standard)** is a third thing: admission control for agent actions (Ed25519 identity, capability authorization, deterministic integer-arithmetic risk evaluation, delegation chains, hash-chained audit) `[snippet]` https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18829. Beware 3-way "ACP" ambiguity in docs; always expand the acronym.

### 4.5 Claude Code hooks and transcript
- Hooks reference `[read]` https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks. Events include SessionStart/End, UserPromptSubmit, Stop, StopFailure, PreToolUse, PostToolUse, PostToolUseFailure, PermissionRequest, PermissionDenied, PostToolBatch, SubagentStart/Stop, TaskCreated/Completed, FileChanged, CwdChanged, PreCompact/PostCompact, Elicitation, InstructionsLoaded, WorktreeCreate/Remove, ConfigChange, and others. Common input: `session_id`, `transcript_path`, `cwd`, `permission_mode`, `hook_event_name`, `agent_id`/`agent_type` (subagents), `prompt_id`, `effort`. PostToolUse adds `tool_name`, `tool_input`, `tool_use_id`, `tool_response`, `tool_response_is_error` (NOTE: field is `tool_response`, not `tool_output` as some blog snippets claim). `Stop` provides `last_assistant_message`.
- **Caveat that matters for Agentvow: `transcript_path` is written asynchronously and may lag the current turn**; use `last_assistant_message` on Stop. Transcript = JSONL under `~/.claude/projects/...` `[snippet]`; its line schema is undocumented in what I read (treat as unstable, version-detect).
- Hooks can **modify** things: PreToolUse `permissionDecision` allow/deny/ask/defer, `updatedInput`, PostToolUse `updatedToolOutput`. Agentvow's hook adapter must be observe-only (human-agency rule); the capability to alter tool output is a risk to flag in the threat model, and also means a transcript may not equal what the tool actually returned.
- Independence note: transcript and hooks are agent-process-adjacent evidence; they capture what the agent harness saw, not independent system state (matches backcheck limitation in matrix).

## VERDICT
1. Human factors: the literature supports H2-style concern but its effect sizes are mixed: explanations often fail to improve (and can worsen) appropriate reliance (Bansal 2021, snippet), cognitive forcing reduces overreliance at an acceptability cost (Buçinca 2021, read abstract, N=199), and explanations help only when they lower verification cost on hard tasks (Vasconcelos 2023, snippet). The lone first-party agent datum is Anthropic's 93% prompt-approval rate (read). A fabricated-looking "13.6% vs 89%" figure circulating in coverage is not in the Anthropic source; exclude it.
2. The research question's clause "without increasing review burden" collides with the best-supported mitigation (forcing functions). The study must measure both and may need to report a trade-off.
3. Visualization SOTA is process-centric with weak evaluations (N=5 studies, comprehension speed). No claim-vs-reality view found in this pass; label as "Potential gap requiring further verification."
4. Standards: no new format needed. in-toto Statement with custom predicate types + CloudEvents transport + PROV export + SARIF ingestion + RATS role vocabulary covers snapshot/collector/hash/freshness. Independence-as-vector has no standard home; define a custom predicate field. Sigstore optional.
5. Protocols: OTel GenAI is still Development (adapter only, pin versions); MCP is at 2026-07-28 (stateless, extensions, sampling/roots/logging deprecated); A2A under Linux Foundation; "ACP folded into A2A" = IBM's Agent Communication Protocol only; Zed's Agent Client Protocol is separate and active; a third "Agent Control Protocol" (arXiv 2603.18829) exists. Claude Code transcript is async (may lag) and hooks can rewrite tool output: transcript is agent-adjacent evidence, not independent evidence.
6. Policy/runtime-verification (Dogwood, VIGIL-2606.26524, ACP, OPA/Cedar) is crowded and gates actions; import as deterministic checkers, do not rebuild. Theory of Code Space supports the premise that agent architectural beliefs drift.
7. Status caveat: most items here are `[snippet]` or abstract-level `[read]`; none is paper-ready.
