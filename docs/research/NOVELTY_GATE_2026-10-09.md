## What this changes
1. **The engineering core is not novel and is crowded**: rule-based claim extraction, rule-engine verdicts with an inconclusive/refused class, staleness by commit+dirty hash, Stop-hook block-once feedback, running tests to check a "tests pass" claim, baseline-aware differential testing. At least three open-source projects (agent-verify, truth, ProofRun) predate or parallel Agentvow's equivalent parts. **Do not describe any of these parts as new.**
2. **What Agentvow actually adds, as engineering combination** (not claims of novelty): a sandboxed differential test run (macOS + Linux) used *as the claim checker*; Copilot agentStop/transcript adapter; per-turn change tracking; verdict view with plain-language "what approval would and would not mean"; signed results; the GitHub Action. Whether any of these is unique is unverified (agent-verify's sandboxing and Copilot support were not checked in depth).
3. **Remaining potentially-novel questions are empirical, not mechanistic:**
   - Does showing claim-vs-reality evidence (with UNKNOWN) change human approval of wrong agent output? The one relevant study found (FSE 2025, 385 participants) varied complexity and AI provenance, **not** verification evidence; no controlled study varying evidence was found in a limited search. Potential gap requiring further verification.
   - How often do real agent PRs with false "tests pass" statements exist, and how often can a local differential run catch them? (Detection study v2 below.) Gong et al. measure inconsistency differently; the execution-based measure appears unreported. Potential gap requiring further verification.
   - Whether the `--new-test-failures review` policy generalises (fresh-sample test).
4. The "decision-gated UNKNOWN / counter-state search" mechanism from the master plan was **not built** and stays a research hypothesis; the shipped UNKNOWN is the common "inconclusive" class.

## Update: detection study v2 result (same day)
`DETECTION_V2.md`: on 140 fresh agent PRs, 13 CI-failing PRs reached a usable local run; 2 were CONTRADICTED (hand-checked real regressions), 4 flagged with the `review` policy, 0 declared fine, 1 environment-caused false alert in 14 controls. This is an execution-based measurement of how often a false "tests pass" statement on a real agent PR can be caught locally; no equivalent execution-based figure was found in the literature pass (Gong et al. measure message-code inconsistency with semantic similarity), so it remains a **potential gap requiring further verification**, not a novelty claim. It is small (13 usable CI-failed PRs, 8 repositories) and clustered.
