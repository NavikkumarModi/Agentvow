# Agentvow: vision and strategy note (draft for the owner, 2026-10-13)

Status: a thinking document, not a plan. Numbers come from this repository's studies; anything about other products is secondary and unverified (see `docs/research/forecast_grounding/PRIOR_ART_2026-10-10.md` and `GAP_ANALYSIS_VOLUME.md`). Nothing here claims novelty: where this note says "gap", read "potential gap requiring further verification".

## 1. The problem in one paragraph
Software changes are about to be written faster than humans can read them. Version control, pull requests and review were built for human-authored changes and human attention. When an agent says "the tests pass", the person reading cannot see the conditions that make that true, and at volume they cannot read the diffs either. The question is what stands between "see only the outcome" (Claude Code style) and "read every line" (Git, PRs, editors) when the human stays at a high level.

## 2. What we actually know (measured, small, honest)
- **Reachability is the gate.** Only 11% of real agent PRs reached a usable independent run with zero-config reconstruction (19% with a sensitivity arm). A verifier cannot close what it cannot replay.
- **Recording the environment at claim time works.** On a random sample (B6, pre-registered), capturing the agent's Python packages reproduced 20 of 22 valid passing runs (91%), against 12 of 22 (55%) for reconstruction from the repository; capture alone reproduced 8, reconstruction alone 0. Limits: one model, 22 runs, Python packages only, reproduction is not correctness, capture is partly an upper-bound control.
- **Agent-declared recipes were fragile** in selected cohorts (omitted test runners, vocabulary rejections) and did not beat repository replay there.
- **Passing is not coverage.** About half of 31 honest, evidence-closed patches contained changed lines that no independent test executed (post-hoc, small patches).
- **Instrument defects are real.** A fixed size guard once turned build failures into "missing dependency" verdicts; fixes were found only on real repositories.
What we do not know: whether humans are overwhelmed in the way assumed, whether evidence-based routing cuts human load without missing defects, and how far capture goes beyond Python packages.

## 3. Three candidate futures
### A. The sharp verifier (a feature-level bet)
*Bet:* Agentvow stays a focused, trustworthy "did the agent's claim hold, independently" tool, improved by capture-at-claim and changed-line coverage.
*For:* shortest path; evidence already supports the core; no dependence on how the industry shifts; useful to every developer who uses agents today.
*Against:* the forges (GitHub, GitLab) and agent vendors could ship the same checks inside their products; value may be a feature, not a company.
*Falsified if:* a forge ships independent replay with captured environments, or users do not act on UNKNOWN/coverage output.
*Next 90 days:* changed-line coverage in every report; capture-at-claim in the Stop hook with replay from the capsule; clearer guard errors (done); a real-user pilot with 5-10 developers.
### B. The attention layer (a layer-level bet): evidence-routed review at agent scale
*Bet:* the missing middle between outcome and diff is a layer that groups changes into decisions and routes each one to leave-alone / escalate / contradicted by independent evidence and its unknowns, under a policy the human sets; the human is never bypassed (no silent approval).
*For:* it matches the owner's reading of the problem; triage by effort exists (arXiv 2601.00753, `[snippet]`) but we found nothing that routes by evidence of correctness (a potential gap); our measurements already provide the two inputs (replayability, per-change coverage) and the verdict model with UNKNOWN.
*Against:* the router can only leave alone what evidence closes, so with Python-package capture it would escalate many changes today; defects needed for a routing study (ground truth) are hard to obtain; human benefit needs a human study; forges could add routing once evidence exists.
*Falsified if:* on a stream of real attempts, evidence-based routing does not beat effort-based routing at the same review budget on recall of real defects, or the closure share stays too low to matter.
*Next 90 days:* the multi-change digest (closed / unknown / contradicted, plain-language reasons, human policy file); a pre-registered replay study over a stream of attempts with injected and natural defects; a small human study only after the replay study shows promise.
### C. The new substrate (a platform-level bet)
*Bet:* agent-native version control and evidence (Git replaced or deeply extended: change graphs, intents, evidence, promotion) becomes the way software is made.
*For:* if humans truly stay high-level, the substrate may need to change; some products point this way (Atomic, GitLab and GitHub agent work; `[read-html]`/`[snippet]` only).
*Against:* crowded and infrastructure-heavy; incumbents have distribution; our evidence says nothing about whether a new substrate is required, only that a verification layer is useful; large research and engineering cost for one person.
*Falsified if:* the gap analysis finds that an extension of existing forges covers the properties (the likely outcome on current evidence).
*Next 90 days:* none recommended; revisit after B has results.

## 4. Recommendation
Do A now and B as the research spine; treat C as a hypothesis to test, not a project. Concretely: ship changed-line coverage and capture-at-claim (both need no new research), run the real-user pilot, then build the digest and pre-register the routing study. Each step must ship value alone, so nothing depends on the big claim succeeding.

## 5. Decisions only the owner can make
1. **Audience:** individual developers, teams, or organisations with agent fleets? It changes the product and the study design.
2. **Open source posture:** the tool is MIT and public; is the goal reputation and adoption, a company, or research? It decides how much of B stays open.
3. **Model scope:** all results are Claude-only. Is a second model family worth the cost before a bigger claim?
4. **Time and money:** the routing study needs ground-truth defects and agent runs (order of tens to a hundred dollars per stage) plus human time for a pilot.
5. **Paper strategy:** the current paper (one study, capture) is small by design; the routing study could be the larger second paper.

## 6. Non-goals
No new version-control system; no silent approval or merging; no claim that reproduction equals correctness; no LLM in any verdict; no novelty claims until the prior-art checks in `PRIOR_ART_2026-10-10.md` are finished.

## 7. What would change this note
A forge shipping independent replay with captured environments; a routing study showing evidence-based routing no better than effort-based; capture failing on system-level state in a way that caps closure well below half; or real users ignoring escalations.

## 8. Update 2026-10-20 (after the mutation line, the real-bug feasibility screens, the source-level prior-art reads and the adversarial review)
Status: still a thinking document. Numbers are from this repository's studies; statements about other products rest on their READMEs and papers (`PRIOR_ART_2026-10-10.md`, update 4) and are not independent evaluations.

**What changed since section 2.**
- *Mechanism is occupied.* Checking claims with fixed rules and binding them to recorded runs is done by `truth`, ProofRun, Proofrail and Trial (read from their READMEs). This note and the paper claim no new verification mechanism.
- *A possible remaining gap, unverified.* As described in their READMEs, none of the four re-executes the result in a separate clean environment and compares execution contexts. This is an absence claim from README text; two tools (Tycho, x-harness) could not be resolved, and searches may have missed work. Read it as a potential gap requiring further verification.
- *Supported by our data, at feasibility scale.* Recording the agent's Python packages when it ran its tests reproduced 20 of 22 valid passing results (91%) against 12 of 22 (55%) for a weak zero-config reconstruction (one model, Python only; the comparator misses are mostly "found nothing to install"; capture is an upper bound on replayability, not a verification method).
- *Not shown.* That mutation survivors predict real defects (29.6% of executed-line mutants survive the target tests, and 74 of 75 checked survivors survive the full suite, but no real defect link). Both routes to ground truth failed: later fixes gave 16 events in 4 repositories, and the real-bug benchmark did not reach its feasibility rule on this machine in two screens (7 and 16 feasible instances against 30 required, in 2 and 3 projects).

**Effect on the three futures.**
- *A (the sharp verifier):* strengthened as the identity, with a narrower statement of what it is: an independent clean-room replay of the agent's claim, using an environment recorded at claim time, that reports UNKNOWN when it cannot reproduce and never says the result is correct.
- *B (the attention layer):* its falsification test needs ground-truth defects, and the two ways we tried to obtain them failed. B is blocked on data, not on the idea. Do not build the routing digest as a research claim until a source of real defects exists. A separately planned feasibility study on historically compatible Linux environments is the honest route; it needs its own stop rule written beforehand.
- *C (new substrate):* unchanged: not recommended.

**Product direction (recommendation, for the owner to accept or change).**
1. Position the product as the reproducibility check: capture at claim time, clean-room replay, the not-reproduced manifest, changed-line coverage, and explicit UNKNOWN. State on the product what it does not do (it does not judge correctness; it covers Python packages, not system packages, services, generated files or secrets).
2. Keep mutation, if offered, as an opt-in "executed but not noticed by the tests" action, never as a defect finding. No mutation feature is added on the present evidence.
3. Do not extend the claim beyond replayability until a strong reconstruction baseline, a larger and more varied sample, cross-time and cross-machine replay, and a case where replay changes a decision exist (the missing evidence listed in the paper).
4. No new version-control substrate.

**Largest risk, unchanged.** Forges or agent vendors could ship independent replay with captured environments inside their products; that is the first falsifier in section 7. A second risk is that users ignore UNKNOWN output; only the pilot can show that, and the pilot needs participants the owner recruits.

**Decisions still the owner's** (section 5, unanswered): audience (individual developers, teams or fleets), open-source posture and goal (reputation, company or research), whether a second model family is worth the cost, time and money, and paper strategy (a short feasibility paper first, targeted at a short work-in-progress track; the evidence listed above would be needed for a full paper).

**What would change this update.** The adversarial review's conclusion that the current paper is a feasibility result, not a full contribution; a forge or vendor shipping the same replay; a Linux real-bug study that reaches its feasibility rule and shows coverage-reassured defects; or capture failing on system-level state so that most claims stay unreproducible.
